Overheard vs Biscotti

Checked against Biscotti's GitHub repository on 31 October 2026

Biscotti and Overheard are both free, native Mac apps that record your calls without a bot, keep the audio, and label speakers. Biscotti runs every step — transcription, speaker identification and summaries — with local models on your Mac. Overheard can transcribe locally too, but hands summaries and questions to Claude, and is built so Claude Code, Claude Desktop and Codex can work with your meetings.

Pick Biscotti if…

nothing may ever leave your Mac, including summaries; you want speakers recognized by voice across meetings, calendar-aware prompts, and you have macOS 15 and 16 GB of RAM.

Pick Overheard if…

you live in Claude Code, Claude Desktop or Codex, want an open-source MIT license, the option of cloud transcription for the best accuracy, or you're still on macOS 14.

Where Biscotti is better

Fully local, summaries included. Biscotti runs its language model on your Mac too, so it works offline end to end. Overheard's on-device path covers transcription; for summaries you choose Apple Intelligence or a Claude or Codex subscription.

Knows who's talking. Biscotti matches voiceprints against past meetings and uses calendar attendees to put real names on speakers. Overheard labels speakers per meeting; naming them is up to you.

Calendar and auto-stop. Biscotti reads your calendar to offer recording when a meeting starts, and stops when the call ends. It also supports custom vocabulary for names and jargon.

Where Overheard is better

Built around Claude. Overheard's MCP server can also act — start or stop a recording, add a bookmark, tick off an action item — and the Claude Code plugin adds /overheard:recap, /overheard:followup and /overheard:todo. Read how it works.

Open source, MIT. Biscotti's source is public under the PolyForm Perimeter license, which limits using it to build a competing product. Overheard is MIT: fork it, change it, ship it.

Cloud when accuracy matters. For hard audio, accents or many languages, Overheard can send a meeting to ElevenLabs Scribe with your own key. Local stays the default if you want it.

Runs on more Macs. Recording works from macOS 14.2, with no RAM recommendation, because the heavy language model isn't on your machine.

Using both

Both just listen to your Mac's audio, so you can try them side by side on the same call and keep the one whose transcripts you prefer.
