Back up Mac meeting recordings to Google Drive

How Overheard's cloud storage works · 7 min read

Overheard can archive Mac meeting recordings and transcripts in your Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, iCloud Drive or WebDAV account. After Overheard verifies the upload, it can free local space. Select Download on an archived meeting to restore it to your Mac.

Cloud storage is optional and off by default. If you never turn it on, nothing changes.

How verified cloud backup works

  1. You pick a place: Dropbox, Google Drive, or your own server.
  2. Overheard uploads each finished meeting there: the audio, plus the transcript, summary and notes.
  3. It checks every upload. It asks the cloud service for a fingerprint of the file and compares it with the one on your Mac. A file only counts as safely stored when they match.
  4. You can free up space. Overheard verifies remote copies before freeing local space. An archived meeting stays listed in your library so you can download its files again.
  5. You can get the audio back any time, with one click.

Your files go to your account. Overheard has no server of its own and never sees your files.

How to connect Google Drive and turn on sync

  1. Open Settings → Cloud storage.
  2. Under Store in, pick Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive or iCloud Drive.
  3. Click Connect and approve in your browser. iCloud Drive needs nothing: it's ready if iCloud Drive is on.
  4. Return to Overheard and check the connection status. If your build asks for OAuth credentials, use a build with the provider configured or supply your own desktop client credentials.
  5. Turn on Sync meetings.

That's it. Overheard starts uploading, newest meetings first. The first upload can take a while if you have many recordings. You can keep using the app, and click Stop at any time.

Where are my files? In Dropbox, under Apps › Overheard. In Google Drive, in an Overheard folder in My Drive. In OneDrive, under Apps › Overheard. In iCloud Drive, in an Overheard folder at the top level (~/Library/Mobile Documents/com~apple~CloudDocs/Overheard). Each meeting has its own folder. Dropbox and OneDrive use app-folder permissions. Google Drive uses the per-file permission drive.file. See the Overheard Privacy Policy for Drive access, retention and revocation.

Everyday use

Right-click a meeting in the library:

ActionWhat happens
Sync nowUploads this meeting right away.
Free up audio spaceRemoves this meeting's audio from your Mac. It stays in the cloud.
Download audioBrings the audio back to your Mac.
Keep on this MacThis meeting's audio is never removed automatically.
Remove cloud copy…Deletes the cloud copy and keeps everything on your Mac.

Cloud-only meetings remain visible in the library. Select Download to restore the files. Once a transcript is on your Mac, you can read it, search it or ask Claude about the meeting with MCP.

Each meeting shows its status, such as Synced, Audio in the cloud or Waiting to sync.

Can I switch cloud services?

Yes. Choose the new service in Settings > Cloud storage and connect it. Keep the previous account connected until the transfer finishes. Overheard asks how to transfer your meetings:

  • Copy to new destination keeps the previous cloud copies.
  • Move to new destination removes the previous copies only after all tracked files have been verified in the new service.
  • Cancel leaves the transfer pending. No previous cloud files are removed.

Files already on your Mac are uploaded directly. Files stored only in the previous cloud service are downloaded first. Keep enough free space for these downloads. If a transfer is interrupted, choose Copy or Move again to resume.

Overheard also checks the cloud copies now and then. A file that someone deleted in the cloud is not uploaded again on its own: the meeting shows Deleted from the cloud and you choose Upload to the cloud again, or Remove cloud copy… to keep it only on your Mac. Use Sync now for an immediate check. A file edited in the cloud is never overwritten: it is saved next to yours as a conflict copy (see below).

How do I open the cloud folder?

In a meeting, click the circular folder button, then Open cloud folder. Show in Finder still opens the local folder. The share menu also offers Copy cloud folder link. These folder links support Dropbox and Google Drive, require access to your account, and do not make the meeting public.

Is my audio safe?

Overheard follows a few strict rules:

  • It never deletes the only copy. Audio leaves your Mac only after the cloud service confirms it holds exactly the same file.
  • It never touches a meeting in use. Recording, transcribing, playing or exporting blocks cleanup.
  • The local library keeps a record of cloud-only meetings. Download a meeting to read its archived transcript or play its recording.
  • Deleting is never final. A file missing in the cloud never deletes anything on your Mac. When you delete a meeting on your Mac, its cloud copies go to the service's trash, not into thin air, and Overheard never empties that trash. Removing a cloud copy by hand is a separate step that you confirm.
  • It never removes a file someone else edited. A cloud file is moved to the trash only if it is still exactly what Overheard uploaded.
  • You get the original back. With the recommended format, Overheard checks before uploading that the compressed copy rebuilds your audio exactly, sample for sample. Only Maximum savings gives back a close copy instead, and the setting says so.
  • Audio you just downloaded stays. It won't be cleaned up again right away.
  • Your sign-in stays private. It is kept in the macOS Keychain, and you can disconnect from Settings at any time.

When something goes wrong

Overheard retries by itself. You don't need to do anything when:

  • You're offline. Syncing resumes when you're back online.
  • An upload stops halfway, even if you quit the app. It continues from where it stopped instead of starting over.
  • You're on a phone hotspot or in Low Data Mode. Automatic syncing pauses to save data. Click Sync now if you want it anyway.

“Possible conflict” copies

If your ~/Meetings folder is inside Dropbox, iCloud Drive or Syncthing, those apps sometimes create duplicates when the same file changes on two devices, such as transcript (Mac's conflicted copy).md or transcript 2.md.

Overheard marks these as Possible conflict instead of showing them as new meetings. Right-click the meeting and choose Review conflict copies. For each copy you can:

  • Use this copy: it replaces the original, and the original goes to the Trash.
  • Keep both: the copy is renamed and kept.
  • Move copy to Trash.

Nothing is decided for you, and anything removed goes to the Trash so you can recover it. Until you choose, that meeting isn't uploaded or cleaned up.

Deleting a meeting

When you move a meeting to the Trash on your Mac, the dialog tells you that its cloud copies go to the service's trash too. Overheard queues them, so it also works if you are offline or quit the app: they are moved the next time it can reach the service. Where to find them:

ServiceWhere the copies wait
Google DriveTrash

Overheard never empties these places: that is up to you or the service. If a server cannot move files to a trash, the cloud copy is simply not removed. The same applies to the automatic cleanup set to Delete everything after some days. Its audio only mode stays off while cloud storage is on, because freeing audio belongs to the verified cleanup above.

Using two Macs

Use the same account and the same cloud folder on both. If the same file changed on both Macs, neither version is overwritten: the cloud's version is saved next to yours as transcript (conflicted copy MacName date).md, syncing that meeting waits, and Review conflict copies lets you Use this copy or Keep both. Just before replacing a file, Overheard checks again that nobody changed it meanwhile. If two Macs connect to the same empty iCloud folder at the very same moment, the one whose marker was set aside adopts the other's library by itself.

Downloads and uploads, and how to stop them

Every transfer shows what it is doing: the file, how much is done out of the total (for example 40 MB / 125 MB), a bar with the percentage, the speed and an estimate of the time left. This is the same for an upload and for Download and play. Where a service cannot report bytes as they arrive (a synced folder copying a file), you see the file and its size, without the bar.

  • Stop (the stop button next to the progress, or Stop next to Downloading in the meeting) cancels that transfer. A download that is stopped leaves nothing half-written on your Mac, and a meeting is not marked as failed because you stopped it. Uploads continue from where they stopped next time.
  • Transfers (N) opens the list of everything queued or running: each meeting with what is happening to it, the progress of the one in progress, a Cancel for each and Stop all. Use it when you started several downloads and want to drop some.

Reading the progress

The bar shows real numbers, not which meeting it is at. With Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive, WebDAV and S3 it counts the bytes still to upload: meetings already in the cloud cost nothing, so a new Sync now does not start over from zero. With iCloud Drive Overheard only copies the files into the folder and macOS does the upload afterwards, so the sync stays in progress until the system reports everything uploaded, with a line like “iCloud has uploaded 120 of 340 files”. Audio leaves your Mac only after that.

Other services

  • Custom WebDAV: enter your server’s HTTPS folder address, username and app password. Overheard checks the connection right away.

Questions

Does Overheard need to stay open? Uploads happen while the app is running, which it always is if you leave it in the menu bar.

How do I get everything back? In Settings → Cloud storage → More: Download all audio to this Mac brings back the audio that's only in the cloud, and Export all meetings to ZIP writes one file with every meeting, cloud-only audio included, without filling your Mac's meeting folder. Meetings left on an older service have the same options under Manage.

What if I trash a meeting by mistake, or lose my Mac's disk? Overheard keeps a map of every recording it sent to the cloud, outside the meetings folder (and in your Time Machine backups): which service, which files, and how to check them. In Settings → Cloud storage → More, Recover meetings from the cloud lists the meetings that are no longer on your Mac and rebuilds any of them from the cloud, with the original audio.

What if I turn cloud storage off? Nothing is deleted, on your Mac or in the cloud. Audio already in the cloud stays there; turn cloud storage back on to download it.

Can I switch from Dropbox to Google Drive later? Yes. When you pick a new place, Overheard asks once what to do with the meetings already stored in the old one, naming both: Copy them (they're on both, so you have two copies), Move them, like cut and paste (each file is removed from the old place, with the meeting's emptied folder, only after it's checked in the new one; if the old service is unreachable at that moment, Overheard finishes the job when it's back), or Keep them where they are (only new meetings go to the new place, and you can still download the old ones from where they are). Your choice is remembered, and Settings shows it with a Change button. It works one meeting at a time: audio that's only in the cloud is downloaded, copied and freed again, so your Mac never needs room for the whole archive. If you no longer have access to the old service, meetings that are fully on your Mac still move over.

Why does a meeting say “Deleted from the cloud”? Its files were deleted or trashed in the cloud, by you or someone else. Overheard does not put them back on its own. Choose Upload to the cloud again or Remove cloud copy… from the meeting's menu.

Does Overheard empty the cloud trash? Never. Deleted meetings wait there, and you restore or purge them with the service.

Why is “Sync meetings” grayed out? No account is connected for the service you picked. Connect it first. If it was already on when the account disappeared, you can still turn it off.

The bar is full but iCloud still shows clouds in Finder. Overheard has copied everything; macOS uploads afterwards. The line under the bar counts the files iCloud has really uploaded, and the sync ends when it reaches all of them.

What if two Macs edit at exactly the same time? Before replacing a file Overheard checks again that it is unchanged, and with Dropbox the service itself refuses the overwrite. With Google Drive and OneDrive, a version that lands in the very last moment before the upload is committed can still be replaced; the earlier one stays in the service's version history.

Is anything not covered? The things in the “not included” column above. In particular: no live co-editing, no automatic merge, no emptying of any trash, and OneDrive's own “name-computer” conflict copies are not recognised yet.

Does Overheard send my meetings anywhere else? No. Only to the place you choose.
